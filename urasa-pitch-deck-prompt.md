## Brand facts — use these exactly, don't invent new ones

**Name & tagline:** Urasa (उरसा). Tagline: "We care about warmth." Standing line: "Seasonal Indian cooking, plated with care." / "Catering for occasions of consequence."

**One-line description:** A seasonal, ingredient-first catering house — one kitchen team, from the first tasting to the last course, built around what the market actually has that week.

**Headline stats:** 6 cities · 10–800 guests per event · 640+ events catered.

**Three principles (the philosophy — this is the core narrative spine of the deck):**
1. **Ritu (ऋतु) — Cook with the season.** Menus rotate through six ritu (traditional Indian seasons), not a fixed banquet calendar or the four Western seasons.
2. **Santulan (संतुलन) — Balance on the plate.** Every course balances grains, protein, and vegetables for flavour and nutrition together — not just size or spectacle.
3. **Nishtha (निष्ठा) — One kitchen, start to finish.** The chefs who plan a menu are the chefs who cook and serve it on the day. No sub-contracted staff, ever.

**The six ritu (for a "seasonal menu" slide):** Vasant (Feb–Apr), Grishma (Apr–Jun), Varsha (Jun–Aug, monsoon), Sharad (Aug–Oct), Hemant (Oct–Dec), Shishir (Dec–Feb). Each has its own produce and cooking instinct — what's right in Vasant isn't what's right two months later.

**Sample six-course sequence (illustrative — real event menus are tasted and confirmed before booking):** Swagat (welcome) → Kachumber (raw salad) → Sabzi (vegetable) → Dal-Chawal (lentils & rice) → Tandoor (from the clay oven) → Mishti (something sweet).

**Services:**
- Corporate & Private Catering — personalised seasonal menus, live kitchen counters
- Event Delivery & Staffing — full-service delivery, front-of-house staffing, end-to-end on-site execution

**Who Urasa serves (use as a 2x2 or list):**
- Corporate & Institutions — 10–250 guests — board dinners, product launches, offsite retreats
- Weddings — 50–500 guests — tasting-led planning from first meeting to final course
- Private Functions — 2–40 guests — in-home tasting menus, milestone celebrations, dedicated on-site chef
- Standing & Reception — 40–800 guests — the same seasonal menu, reformatted as a canapé sequence for a moving room

**Responsibility (good as a "why it's different" or trust-building slide):**
- Seasonal, close sourcing — short supply chains, nothing forced out of season
- Balance on every plate — real nutrition, portioning that respects the guest
- Minimal, deliberate waste — headcounts confirmed close to the date, tasting surplus redirected to staff meals, never discarded
- One accountable kitchen — every person on site is an Urasa hire, trained and scheduled directly, never outsourced

**Client testimonials (use 2–3 as social proof, verbatim):**
- "The pacing was the thing our guests still mention. Nobody was waiting on a course, and nothing felt rushed either." — Priya Nair, Head of Events, Meridian Capital
- "We changed our headcount twice in the final week and the team absorbed it without a single visible ripple on the day." — Thomas Berg, Wedding client, June 2025
- "Genuinely seasonal — the menu we tasted in March was not the menu we ate in May, and it was better for it." — Ananya Rao, Founder, Studio Rao

**How booking works (closing slide):** Share your date, headcount, and occasion → request a tasting → menu tasted and confirmed together → Urasa's own team executes the event start to finish.

## Suggested structure (adjust as needed)

1. Title — logo, tagline "We care about warmth," one-line description
2. The idea — what makes Urasa different in one sentence (seasonal, one accountable kitchen)
3. Philosophy — Ritu, Santulan, Nishtha as three pillars
4. Seasonal menu — the six ritu + why "seasonal" is a real constraint, not decoration
5. A sample sequence — the six-course structure with a couple of real dish photos
6. Services — the two service lines
7. Who we serve — the four formats with guest ranges
8. Why one kitchen matters — Nishtha expanded: no sub-contracted staff, real ownership
9. Responsible by default — the four responsibility pillars
10. Proof — 2–3 testimonials
11. By the numbers — 6 cities / 10–800 guests / 640+ events
12. How it works — the booking process, ending on a clear call to action ("Request a tasting")
13. Contact / closing — logo, tagline, contact prompt

## Visual direction

Primary palette: brand green `#40492C` (deep `#2E3320`), warm paper cream `#EDEEE8`, with indigo `#2B3A55` and gold `#9C8248` as small accents — this is the site's actual brand identity, matching the logo. Headings in a serif (Shippori Mincho if available, otherwise a comparable elegant serif); body text in a clean sans (Manrope or comparable). Generous whitespace, one strong photo or one clear idea per slide — avoid dense bullet walls. Devanagari marks (ऋतु, संतुलन, निष्ठा, उरसा) can appear as small typographic accents next to their English equivalents, matching how the brand uses them on its own site.

## Before building

If the prospective client's industry/occasion type (corporate vs. wedding vs. private) isn't specified when this prompt is used, ask — it changes which "who we serve" segment and testimonial to lead with. Otherwise keep the deck adaptable to any of the four formats.
